\section{Validated reconstruction of the historical catalogue}\label{sec:catalogue}

\subsection{Scope and provenance}

The release contains 47 catalogue entries associated with the historical
EXPINT method set~\cite{BerlandSkaflestadWright2007}, including Runge--Kutta,
Lawson, exponential Adams, predictor--corrector and general linear, affine
Lie-group and generalized Lawson schemes, and the retained Crank--Nicolson
comparator.  The full metadata table appears in Supplementary Table~S1.  The
objective of the reconstruction was not to copy the historical MATLAB routines
line for line, but to encode the method intended by the published construction
and to retain enough provenance to explain every canonical decision.

For families that can be generated from interpolation or moment conditions,
the construction uses exact rational arithmetic for polynomial coefficients
and converts only the final scalar multipliers of analytic operator expressions
to floating point.  This reduces the transcription surface and makes
neighbouring members of a family share one derivation.  The generalized and
modified Lawson families of Krogstad~\cite{Krogstad2005} and Ostermann,
Thalhammer and Wright~\cite{OstermannThalhammerWright2006}, for example, are
generated from one construction rather than maintained as ten unrelated
coefficient tables.

\subsection{Verification hierarchy}

A historical conflict was not resolved by choosing whichever version produced
the most attractive convergence plot.  The audit used a hierarchy of evidence:
structural dimensions and explicitness; coefficient identities and zero-operator
limits; published stage nodes and quadrature constructions; strong and weak
operator-valued order conditions when available; numerical regressions; and,
finally, execution of the original MATLAB files.  The resulting record
separates an executable discrepancy from a metadata or semantic discrepancy.
Table~\ref{tab:reconciliation} lists the five executable or regenerated cases
and Table~\ref{tab:reconciliation-meta} the six metadata cases retained in the
main paper.

\subsection{Cross-check against the original sources}\label{sec:crosscheck}

The private companion supplies the drivers, archived numerical outputs and
summary of the comparison with the 2005 EXPINT files.  The original MATLAB
sources are not redistributed; seven source-file SHA-256 values identify the
five discrepant scheme files and the two common routines listed in the
cross-check record.  The recorded interpreter is GNU Octave 8.4.0.  The script
\path{software/tools/expint_crosscheck/crosscheck_expint.m} specifies both
problems: $y'=-y+y^2$, $y(0)=0.4$, with exact startup, and a two-dimensional
system with
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0.3\\0.2&-2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 N(y)=y^2,\qquad y_0=(0.4,0.3)^T,
\]
where squaring is componentwise and startup is numerical.  Both use
$[0,1.6]$ and $h=0.1$.  The CSV contains 102 output rows: two problems for
47 original schemes and four corrected variants.  The Python comparison
driver specifies the matching PyEXPINT computations and its $10^{-12}$
comparison criterion.

The archived comparison reports agreement within that criterion on both
problems for 42 entries, with largest relative differences
$1.3\times10^{-15}$ and $5.9\times10^{-15}$.  ABLawson2, ETD2RK,
ETD5RKF and ETD2CF3 agree after the four documented one-line corrections;
the largest of their eight reported corrected differences is
$2.3\times10^{-14}$.  ETD5RKF already agrees on the matrix problem to
$8.5\times10^{-15}$ while its uncorrected scalar difference is
$6.5\times10^{-3}$.  Unified diffs of the four changes accompany the outputs.
ModGenLawson45 remains distinct: its output coefficients are regenerated
from the published construction.  Six differing coefficient pairs at
$z=-0.3$ are tabulated, but no agreement of a corrected final-state
implementation is inferred for that entry.

Supplementary Table~S6 reproduces the unchanged summary.  Its coefficient
indices are one-based, as specified by the supplied drivers.  Refinement
outputs and an ETD5RKF storage-representation comparison are also archived
separately.  These numerical comparisons support the stated reconstruction;
they are neither new convergence theorems nor certification of all inputs
admitted by the software.

\subsection{Catalogue-wide regression}

The private validation records contain 275 passing tests on the source
snapshot and the same 275 test identifiers against the installed wheel,
under Python 3.11.15, NumPy 1.24.3, SciPy 1.10.1 and pytest 7.1.2.  Of these,
80 exercise the C2--C4 work, adaptive and cloning contracts; the supplied
negative-control log records 53 failures and 27 passes when those tests are
applied to the earlier C1 baseline.  These logs are from the preparation
environment; they are not cluster timing records.  The repository contains
a Python 3.11--3.14 CI configuration, but the supplied candidate logs do not
establish execution of the entire version matrix.

The evidence map \path{reproducibility/coverage/catalogue_test_map.csv}
connects all 47 entries to source definitions, structural validation,
named scalar-order regressions and the linear tests, and lists dedicated
coefficient or moment tests where they exist.  Its README identifies the
JUnit record and the scope of each evidence category.  Moment residuals
evaluated at selected arguments are numerical checks, not proofs of a
functional identity.  The scalar regression accepts the best eligible
adjacent slope; it is not a universal convergence result.  Additional
symbolic constructions are present in the source, but no execution of the
symbolic audit is inferred from the pytest log.

Supplementary Table~S1 records the coefficient provenance, order metadata
and cross-check classification.  The evidence map complements that table
without promoting metadata or the total number of tests into a proof of
all stiff-order assertions.

\subsection{What ``validated'' means here}

Throughout this article, \emph{validated catalogue} means that the release has
a traceable canonical specification, machine-checkable structural and algebraic
checks where implemented, numerical regressions consistent with the recorded
interpretation, and agreement to roundoff with the original implementation
wherever the two are meant to coincide.  It does not mean formal verification
in a proof assistant, nor does it certify every stiff-order assertion for every
infinite-dimensional problem class.  This narrower meaning is the one relevant
to a software artifact intended to preserve and execute a heterogeneous
historical method collection.
