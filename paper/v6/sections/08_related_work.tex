The most direct historical reference is EXPINT
itself~\cite{BerlandSkaflestadWright2007}.  EXPINT was designed to make a broad
set of exponential Runge--Kutta, multistep and general linear formulas easy to
deploy and test in MATLAB, with the matrix-function technology of its period,
Pad\'e approximants of the $\varphi$ functions~\cite{SkaflestadWright2009}.
\pkg{} preserves the value of the broad catalogue but changes the software
decomposition: method coefficients become backend-independent analytic
expressions, all explicit methods pass through one external-state engine, and
large-scale matrix-function-vector algorithms are interchangeable realizations
rather than embedded method code.  The relationship is established by
execution: Section~\ref{sec:crosscheck} runs the original package against the
reconstruction, method by method.

EXPODE pursued another broad-toolbox design in MATLAB and supports several
classes of exponential integrators together with direct and Krylov-based
matrix-function evaluation, including adaptivity for some
methods~\cite{Jansing2014}.  The overlap is conceptual: both projects treat
experimentation across integrator classes as a software goal.  \pkg{} differs
in the unification around an explicit EGLM registry, the reconstructed and
cross-checked EXPINT catalogue, its action algebra, and the work and provenance
instrumentation.

At the matrix-function level, Expokit~\cite{Sidje1998}, Algorithm~919 and
KIOPS are not competitors to the \pkg{} architecture so much as exemplars of
why the action boundary is needed.  Algorithm~919 develops an adaptive Krylov
procedure for combinations of the exponential and $\varphi$
functions~\cite{NiesenWright2012}; KIOPS extends the augmented-Krylov strategy
with adaptive incomplete orthogonalization and substepping for
exponential-integrator workloads~\cite{GaudreaultRainwaterTokman2018}.  The
\pkg{} KIOPS-type backend is an independent implementation derived from the
published algorithmic ideas, which is why it is not called KIOPS, and it is not
claimed to outperform the reference implementation.  The real-Leja backend
follows the interpolation of Caliari, Vianello and
Bergamaschi~\cite{CaliariVianelloBergamaschi2004} with the backward-error view
of Caliari, Kandolf, Ostermann and Rainer~\cite{CaliariKandolfOstermannRainer2016};
LeXInt provides Leja-based exponential Rosenbrock and EPIRK integrators in
Python and a later C++/CUDA
version~\cite{DekaEinkemmerTokman2023,DekaMorigglEinkemmer2023}, and shows that
polynomial actions can be competitive with Krylov methods.  In \pkg{} Leja is
one realization available to the same historical method definitions rather
than the defining representation of the library.

The closest Python neighbour of the adaptive solver is \texttt{rkstiff}, which
implements the ETD34 pair of Whalen, Brio and
Moloney~\cite{WhalenBrioMoloney2015} together with ETD35, IF34 and IF45DP for
diagonal or dense matrix linear operators~\cite{rkstiff}.  \pkg{} implements the same ETD34
estimator for general operators and documents its blindness to
state-independent forcing, which the robustness analysis of Zoto and
Bowman~\cite{ZotoBowman2023} addresses with new pairs.

SciPy provides a widely used exponential-action implementation through
\texttt{expm\_multiply}~\cite{AlMohyHigham2011,VirtanenEtAl2020}.  \pkg{} uses
that functionality in its hybrid realization instead of replacing the
scientific Python ecosystem's mature pure-exponential path.  NumPy and SciPy
also provide the array, dense and sparse, transform and linear-algebra
substrate for the package~\cite{HarrisEtAl2020,VirtanenEtAl2020}.

The Julia ecosystem separates reusable matrix-function actions from
time-integration packages in a way that is closest to the design adopted
here.  ExponentialUtilities.jl supplies matrix exponential and $\varphi$-action
utilities, including KIOPS, for the SciML
integrators~\cite{ExponentialUtilities2026}, and OrdinaryDiffEq.jl offers
Lawson, N{\o}rsett, ETDRK, HochOst4, Exp4, EPIRK and adaptive Exprb schemes
whose actions are either cached dense functions or Krylov evaluations
selected by one option~\cite{OrdinaryDiffEqExponential}.  That switch is the
same separation of method and realization pursued here with six realizations,
an explicit algebra of combinations, fusion and an extension hook.  The niche
of \pkg{} is the combination of a validated historical EGLM registry, one
execution engine, multiple action realizations, startup and history semantics,
and benchmark instrumentation in a Python package.
