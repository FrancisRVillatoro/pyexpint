\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

\pkg{} demonstrates that a broad historical exponential-integrator catalogue
can be represented as mathematical data and executed through a small number of
well-defined software boundaries.  The 47 reconstructed entries share one
explicit exponential general linear engine; analytic $\varphi$ expressions are
separated from their numerical realization; history and startup are explicit;
and work instrumentation is collected at the backend boundary.  This
architecture makes dense, diagonal, Krylov, KIOPS-type, real-Leja, hybrid and
automatic realizations available without duplicating the time integrators
themselves, and admits external realizations through one hook.

The validation work is as important as the abstraction.  The supplied comparison summary reports near-roundoff agreement
with the original EXPINT implementation for 42 entries, agreement after
one-line corrections for four entries, and regenerated output coefficients
for one entry.  Its aggregate and named results, comparison drivers and detailed original
outputs are now archived with the identified private software companion.  Coefficient and metadata inconsistencies are
reconciled by structural and algebraic diagnostics, confirmed by execution and
guarded by regressions.  The fusion ablation shows a concrete benefit of
representing action algebra explicitly, with both Krylov realizations, while
the work--precision and timing experiments show why performance should not be
reduced to a single counter or backend ranking.  The canonical paired timing
experiment favours the KIOPS-type backend for every tested configuration, but
its manufactured solution favours Krylov realizations by construction and the
rule-based selector chooses the slower backend throughout; the appropriate
conclusion is problem- and environment-dependent selection, not a universal
winner.

The embedded ETD34 solver shows how the action boundary serves an adaptive
integrator: matrix-action tolerance is distinct from temporal tolerance,
failed iterative actions and non-finite trials become rejected steps,
termination is explicit, and endpoint nonlinear values are reused.  It also
inherits a limitation of the published pair, an error estimate that vanishes
for state-independent forcing, which is documented rather than hidden, and
the default numerical startup does not in general deliver the nominal orders
of the high-order history methods.  The original broadband record shows a change in the faster realization at
closely comparable accuracy for selected tolerance pairs.  Its workstation
environment, initial/reference states and individual timings are preserved;
the least accurate setting is not a matched-error speed comparison.

The manuscript package makes the supplied numerical summaries and
their postprocessing inspectable.  The identified private candidate, validation and installation logs,
original experiment records and a family-to-test evidence map accompany it.
The claims distinguish recorded software execution, independently
checkable data transformations and limitations that remain outside
those tests.  The next
technical directions are outside the present claims: variable-step history
methods, order-matched numerical startup, robust embedded pairs, data-driven
backend selection, cost models that include orthogonalization and nonlinear
work, and communication-aware distributed action backends.  The catalogue and
the common execution layer provide a controlled platform on which those
extensions can be studied without reopening the mathematical definition of
every method.
