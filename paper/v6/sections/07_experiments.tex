\section{Numerical experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments distinguish three questions: whether the recorded method is
implemented consistently, how algebraic execution choices change its work, and
what a specified execution environment reports about elapsed time.  These
questions require different controls.  Classical and stiff-order experiments
use analytic or directly diagonalized references.  The fusion experiment
changes only one execution option.  The method comparison fixes the action
backend and the numerical integration window.  The canonical timing experiment
retains separate campaigns and paired measurement blocks instead of pooling
all observations into a single ranking.

The supplied numerical records have heterogeneous provenance.
Table~\ref{tab:protocol} distinguishes historical data, author-reported
candidate runs and postprocessing.  Classical and stiff-order regeneration,
the new fusion and generalized Lawson runs, the numerical-startup study and
a differential check of 24 timing configurations are reported in the
candidate description.  Their execution logs and candidate snapshot are
now supplied in the private companion for software-level verification.  The 108-case fixed-step
campaign, canonical timings and forced adaptive comparisons retain their
historical identity; a check of the 24 timing configurations is not a check of
the 108 fixed-step cases.  All plots in this package are generated from the
supplied records.  Regeneration of a plot establishes the data-to-plot
transformation, not reexecution of the underlying integrator.  The original Allen--Cahn record is supplied with the driver and
configuration described in Section~\ref{sec:broadband}.

\subsection{Classical convergence and stiff-order diagnostics}\label{sec:orders}

The classical scalar problem is
\begin{equation}
 y'=-y+y^2,\qquad y(0)=0.4,\qquad
 y(t)=\frac{1}{1+1.5e^t},\qquad 0\le t\le1.6.
\end{equation}
The four step sizes are $0.2,0.1,0.05,0.025$.  The top panel of
Figure~\ref{fig:order} selects ETD4RK, Krogstad, HochOst4 and ETD5RKF.  The
first three exhibit the expected fourth-order refinement, while ETD5RKF
exhibits fifth-order refinement on this example.  The separate final-state
comparison in Section~\ref{sec:crosscheck} does not supply the complete
EXPINT refinement sequence used to compare convergence orders.  This comparison also illustrates why an order
label alone does not rank errors at a specified step: the fifth-order method
need not have the smallest error in the displayed range.  The full catalogue
metadata and a generic convergence test of the generalized Lawson families on
a six-dimensional system are retained in Supplementary Tables~S1 and~S2.

For the stiff tests, the manufactured solution is
$u_*(x,t)=x(1-x)e^t$ on $(0,1)$ with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary values, as
in Hochbruck and Ostermann~\cite{HochbruckOstermann2005}.  The two nonlinear
terms are, respectively, $1/(1+u^2)$ and the spatial integral of $u$, together
with the manufactured source.  The source is formed at the semidiscrete level.
A centred second-difference Laplacian on 200 interior points is diagonalized
by the discrete sine transform of type I, and its $\varphi$-actions are
evaluated with DiagonalBackend.  Thus an iterative Krylov or Leja stopping
tolerance is not part of these temporal refinement curves.  The steps range
from $1/20$ to $1/640$ by successive halving.

The middle and bottom panels of Figure~\ref{fig:order} show that the
classical order does not by itself predict the observed stiff behaviour.  In
the nonlocal test the last adjacent slope of ETD4RK is about 2.50; the
corresponding Krogstad slope is about 3.36, whereas HochOst4 retains
approximately fourth-order behaviour.  These numbers are finite-grid,
finite-step diagnostics, not new order theorems.  Some of the finest errors are
already of order $10^{-13}$, so their last differences should not be used to
upgrade a method classification.  RKMK2e and RKMK4t show approximately
second-order decay in these tests without thereby satisfying the strong
second-order moment condition away from $z=0$.  Their recorded strong and weak
distinction is retained in Table~\ref{tab:reconciliation-meta}.  All plotted points
are preserved; the reference slopes drawn in the panels are guides, not fits.

\subsection{Ablation of algebraic action fusion}\label{sec:fusionexperiment}

The fusion experiment compares one realization with compatible action sums
evaluated independently or jointly.  It uses the unit tridiagonal operator
$\mathrm{tridiag}(1,-2,1)$ of dimension 256, the reaction $N(u)=0.08u^2$, the
initial state $u_0=0.05+0.01\sin s$ with $s$ uniform on $[0,\pi]$, final time
$T=0.3$, step $h=0.05$ and action tolerance $10^{-10}$.  The seven retained
methods include one-step and general linear specifications; their definitions
and the time grid are unchanged by the fusion switch.  Full Arnoldi is switched
with its own fusion flag; the KIOPS-type backend has no switch, and its unfused
path is obtained by hiding the fused operation from the bound context.

Figure~\ref{fig:fusion} reports the counts.  With full Arnoldi, Krogstad
decreases from 756 to 486 products, PEC423 from 774 to 510 and EGLM433 from
990 to 609; across the seven methods the reductions range from 13.4\% to
38.5\%.  With the KIOPS-type backend the reductions range from 11.8\% to
44.2\%.  In every case the fused and unfused final states agree to a relative
difference below $1.3\times10^{-15}$.  The selection contains three methods without history, ETD4RK,
Krogstad and HochOst4, and four with nonlinear history, PEC423, EGLM433,
GenLawson43 and ModGenLawson43.  The archived record supplies aggregate
counts and final-state differences, not projected dimensions for every
action or trials with different initial states.  Equal totals therefore do
not establish vector-independent Krylov dimensions or isolate the cause of
a difference from an earlier run.

The outcome is deliberately stated in terms of the measured work component.  A
smaller count does not imply the same percentage reduction in runtime: forming
and evaluating a larger augmented projected problem has its own cost, and the
development records contain cases in which fusion did not reduce elapsed time.
The portable conclusion is that the generic action boundary can exploit a
semantics-preserving transformation whose computational benefit must be
assessed with more than one observable.

\subsection{Method work--precision with a fixed action backend}\label{sec:methodwp}

The method comparison uses 511 interior points and the nonseparable
manufactured solution
\begin{equation}
 u_*(x,t)=e^t q_1(x)+\tfrac14 e^{2t}q_2(x),\qquad
 q_1=x(1-x),\qquad q_2=x^2(1-x)^2.
\end{equation}
For the discrete Laplacian $L$, the forcing is computed as
$u_*'(t)-Lu_*(t)-1/(1+u_*(t)^2)$, rather than by substituting the continuous
second derivative.  The KIOPS-type action tolerance is fixed at $10^{-13}$.
All methods use exact history and the same numerical integration window
$[0.5,1]$.  Startup history lies before this window and is not presented as
the cost of a practical numerical startup; Section~\ref{sec:startup} shows what
the default numerical startup does to the high-order entries.  The four steps
are $0.1,0.05,0.025,0.0125$.  The campaign reports a semidiscrete residual of
$1.22\times10^{-15}$ and no nonconverged matrix-function action in any of its
108 method--step cases.

Figure~\ref{fig:methodwp} selects ETD4RK, Krogstad, HochOst4, ABNorsett4,
PEC625, PEC726, PECEC635 and PECEC736 from the 27 methods of the campaign.
Every displayed curve contains the four step sizes.  Open enlarged markers
identify points at or below the precision cutoff $5\times10^{-12}$, which is
tied to the action tolerance; these points remain visible but are excluded
from the non-dominated curves.  The cutoff is a diagnostic for this data set,
not a solver tolerance or accuracy guarantee.

The result depends materially on the chosen work observable.  In the
error--matvec plane, the non-dominated subset of the eight displayed methods
starts with the four ABNorsett4 refinements and then contains PEC625 at
$h=0.05$, PEC726 at $h=0.05$, PEC625 at $h=0.025$ and PECEC635 at $h=0.025$.
In the error--nonlinear-evaluation plane, the corresponding sequence contains
ABNorsett4 at $h=0.1$, PEC726 and PECEC736 at $h=0.1$, PEC726 and PECEC736 at
$h=0.05$, and PEC625 and PECEC635 at $h=0.025$.  Thus a method that is
efficient in one recorded component need not be efficient in another.  The
figure is a work decomposition under one fixed matrix-action realization, not
a universal ranking of the time integrators.  Two further observations from
the same campaign are instructive.  ETD5RKF, of classical order five, converges
with adjacent slopes 4.66, 3.27 and 2.97 on this stiff problem, the clearest
illustration in the catalogue of why classical and stiff orders are recorded
separately.  The generalized Lawson entries GenLawson44 and GenLawson45 show
slopes rising towards 5 and 6 here, one above their recorded orders, whereas on
the generic six-dimensional test of Supplementary Table~S2 their slopes
decrease towards the recorded 4 and 5; the recorded orders are kept, and the
parabolic behaviour is reported as a problem-dependent observation.

\subsection{Replicated paired timing}\label{sec:timing}

The canonical timing problem is a periodic two-dimensional manufactured
reaction--diffusion system on $[0,2\pi)^2$, with $\nu=0.02$, $T=0.5$ and
\begin{equation}
 u_*(x,y,t)=0.2e^{-t}\sin(x)\sin(y).
\end{equation}
The spatial operator is the Kronecker-sum second-difference Laplacian; the
nonlinear part is $u^2$ plus the semidiscrete source required by $u_*$.  The
measured operation is the ETD34 solve only: problem construction and explicit
garbage collection are outside the timer.  Each configuration has one warmup
and 12 timed blocks, and the backend order is balanced cyclically.

The grids have $64^2$, $128^2$ and $256^2$ unknowns, and the requested
tolerances are $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-7}$.  The four choices are KIOPS-type, real
Leja, the SciPy/KIOPS hybrid and the rule-based selector.  The absolute
tolerance is $10^{-3}$ times the relative one; the action-tolerance fraction
is 0.01, with the documented clipping rule; the initial step is 0.1 and the
maximum step is 0.2.  Explicit Leja and the Leja realization chosen by the
selector use a maximum degree of 80 without substepping, which covers the
scaled spectral widths of this problem, at most 53 with $h=0.2$ on the finest
grid.  These are experiment settings, not inferred defaults.

The campaigns ran on the Picasso cluster of the University of M\'alaga, as
job 2203999 on node sd041 and jobs 2206865 and 2207024 on node sd004, each on
one allocated core of an Intel Xeon Gold 6230R at 2.10\,GHz with thread counts
set to one, under Python 3.11.4, NumPy 1.24.3 and SciPy 1.10.1.  They are three
separate campaigns, not three independent machine types, and no exclusive node
occupancy is asserted.  For backend $B$, replica $r$, case $c$ and paired
block $b$, the timing observable is
\begin{equation}
 R_{r,c,b}(B)=t_{r,c,b}(B)/t_{r,c,b}(\mathrm{KIOPS}).
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:timing} preserves the three per-replica medians and the
within-replica first and third quartiles for every case.  The 54 medians all
exceed one, between 1.044 and 1.484, so the KIOPS-type backend was faster than
each alternative in these configurations and on this machine.

Three facts qualify that statement.  First, the comparison is at closely
comparable accuracy: the final errors of the four realizations differ from the
KIOPS-type error by ratios between 0.960 and 1.0005 in every configuration,
with the same numbers of nonlinear evaluations, 13 at $10^{-5}$ and 29 at
$10^{-7}$, and of accepted and rejected steps.  The re-run with the submitted
version shows that the step sequences are identical at $10^{-5}$, namely
$0.1,0.2,0.2$, and agree to five decimal places at $10^{-7}$.  Second, the work
counters explain the ordering.  Real Leja performs 1.63 to 3.11 times as many
matrix-vector products as the KIOPS-type backend but takes only 1.08 to 1.47
times as long, so the amortized solve time per recorded product is 0.45 to
0.81 times that of the Krylov realization; this amortized ratio contains all
the work of a solve, not the cost of one product.  The KIOPS-type counts
depend weakly on the grid, 542, 542 and 558 products at $10^{-7}$, whereas the
Leja counts grow with it, 884, 1109 and 1720.  This is consistent with the
nature of the manufactured solution, an eigenvector of the periodic
second-difference Laplacian whose square adds only the Fourier modes of
wavenumber 0 and 2, so that the vectors acted upon are dominated by a few
modes, a situation in which a Krylov space of small dimension captures the
action while a polynomial interpolant on the whole spectral interval must
still resolve the width of the interval.  The campaign therefore does not
inform about operators with broadband data, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:broadband} adds one.  Third, at the looser tolerance the
controller is maximum-step-limited: thirteen evaluations are three attempts,
and with the initial step 0.1 and the maximum step 0.2 the only possible
sequence is $0.1,0.2,0.2$, so half of the campaign exercises the error control
only through the acceptance of those steps.

The selector chose real Leja in all 18 configurations and its medians exceed
those of explicit Leja by 0.3\% to 3.7\% in ratios of unpaired medians; the
bottom panel of Figure~\ref{fig:timing} therefore measures Leja plus whatever
the dispatch adds, without a paired isolation of that overhead.  The
rule-based thresholds are a counterweight to any reading in which automatic
selection must win: on this problem the rule picks the slower backend, and the
calibrated selector of Section~\ref{sec:backends} is not evaluated here.  The
replicas are informative about variability.  Within a replica the interquartile
ranges of the paired ratios are 0.13\% to 2.63\% of the median, but between
replicas the medians differ by 2.6\% to 5.6\%, with the sd041 replica lowest in
14 of 18 cases, while the absolute medians differ by 17\% to 30\% between the
two nodes.  Three campaigns on two nodes do not support a causal attribution
of these differences, but the between-replica spread is a better indication of
the variability of a paired ratio than the within-replica quartiles, and the
conclusion survives it because the smallest median is 1.044.
The complete 72 configuration summaries appear in Supplementary Table~S3 with
errors and work counters beside time.  Hybrid matvecs are not used as a
total-work competitor because the counter omits the internal SciPy work.
Development crossovers measured under other protocols remain in Supplementary
Figure~S1.

\subsection{Adaptive work and achieved accuracy}\label{sec:adaptivework}

The structural comparison in Table~\ref{tab:adaptivity} is complemented by
the four periodic pairs in Table~\ref{tab:etd34}.  The record contains two
requested tolerances per backend and controller.  It does not support a smooth
work--precision curve or a fixed percentage advantage at matched final error.
At rtol $10^{-4}$ the KIOPS-type runs use 731 and 334 matvecs, but their final
errors are approximately $2.01\times10^{-7}$ and $2.30\times10^{-6}$.
\input{table05_etd34.tex}

These comparisons are also exposed to the limitation that
Section~\ref{sec:adaptivity} makes explicit.  The problems are manufactured,
so their source terms are part of $N$.  The direct additive source
cancels from the same-time nonlinear difference used by ETD34, but its
influence through the stage states does not generally cancel, as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:forcing-indirect} shows.  Step doubling compares two complete
step constructions instead.  The records do not isolate the effect: ETD34
reaches larger final errors than step doubling in the KIOPS-type periodic
cases, but smaller ones in the Leja case at $10^{-4}$ and in the nonlocal stiff
test, so an underestimate of the error is a possible contribution to its lower
work, not an established cause.  The broadband problem of
Section~\ref{sec:broadband}, where $N$ depends on the state only, shows the
pair without an additive manufactured source, but does not by itself
prove a robustness property for every autonomous nonlinearity.  The stiff adaptive tests supply a further
qualification.  In the nonlocal problem at rtol $10^{-6}$,
ETD34 records 97 nonlinear evaluations versus 84 for step doubling, while
attaining errors $3.51\times10^{-8}$ and $2.67\times10^{-7}$.  Thus the lower
work per completed trial is not a theorem about total solve work.
Supplementary Table~S4 retains both local and nonlocal tests, including this
less favourable count comparison.

The cost-aware stress test reinforces the same distinction.  Its matvec proxy
decreases by approximately 8.0\% and 21.4\%, yet the recorded wall times
increase by approximately 9.7\% and 3.8\%.  This is not promoted to a
replicated performance result.  It demonstrates why one counter is insufficient
for a cost model that also contains nonlinear evaluations, projected work,
vector operations and implementation overhead.

\subsection{A broadband problem without manufactured forcing}\label{sec:broadband}

The Allen--Cahn equation $u_t=\varepsilon u_{xx}+u-u^3$ on the periodic
interval $[0,1)$ is discretized by second differences on 1024 points, with
$\varepsilon=2.5\times10^{-4}$ and $T=1$.  The initial state is
$u_{0,j}=\tanh(4f_j/\max_k|f_k|)$, where $f$ is a sum of six sine and cosine
modes.  The original record specifies seed 7, all twelve coefficients,
the construction rule and the full initial vector.  The driver is
\path{software/benchmarks/broadband_allen_cahn.py} in the private companion.
The initial and reference-final vectors have six sign changes and require
27 and 18 positive-frequency bins, respectively, to accumulate 99.99\% of
the recorded nonconstant spectral energy.  Thus this experiment is not
confined to the single Fourier mode of the canonical timing problem.

The reference record stores the 1024-component result of fixed-step
HochOst4 with 4096 steps and KIOPS-type action tolerance $10^{-13}$.
It reports an infinity-norm difference of $3.897\times10^{-14}$ from the
2048-step comparison and final maximum amplitude $0.9995349106$.
The finer reference state is supplied; the coarser reference state is not,
so the comparison difference is a retained diagnostic, not independently
recomputed from the two states in this package.  Similarly, per-solve final
errors are outputs of the driver; the record does not retain all 18 final
solution vectors.

ETD34 with KIOPS-type and real-Leja actions is compared with Krogstad step
doubling with KIOPS-type actions, at six relative tolerances from $10^{-4}$
to $10^{-9}$.  The settings are $\mathrm{atol}=10^{-3}\mathrm{rtol}$,
$h_0=0.01$, $h_{\max}=0.2$ and action fraction 0.01.  The original JSON
contains the options, achieved errors, counts, accepted step sequences and
five individual wall times for all 18 configurations.  The five times are
measurements of each complete solve, not paired observations across
configurations.  The recorded execution used Python 3.11.16, NumPy 1.24.3
and SciPy 1.10.1 with single-threaded BLAS on an Intel Core Ultra 9 185H
workstation.  The recorded source commit is
\texttt{f6ef3de1a85005d93f8a3e3744514dce65b9f68a}; it precedes the final
review snapshot, and no new timing of that snapshot is claimed.

Figure~\ref{fig:broadband} uses the full original record rather than its
previous provisional transcription.  At $10^{-4}$, Leja takes 0.631 times
the KIOPS-type time but reaches an error 4.723 times larger: that point is
not a matched-accuracy speed comparison.  At $10^{-5}$, the error and time
ratios are 0.973 and 0.655, while at $10^{-9}$ they are 0.997 and 2.081.
The latter pairs show a change of the faster realization at closely
comparable achieved accuracy within this experiment.  At $10^{-6}$ the
counts are close but not equal, 4891 for Leja and 4954 for KIOPS-type.
Counts, errors and times are distinct observables; the curves do not imply
monotonic changes at every refinement or a universal backend ordering.

\subsection{End-to-end order with numerical startup}\label{sec:startup}

The high-order history entries of Section~\ref{sec:methodwp} were run with
exact history.  Table~\ref{tab:startup} reports what the default numerical
startup does to them on the classical scalar problem of
Section~\ref{sec:orders}, integrated to $t=1$ with exact diagonal actions and
steps from $0.1$ to $0.0125$.  With one HochOst4 step per startup interval, the
selected entries of order six and seven have slopes between 4.55 and
4.80 with numerical startup, compared with 5.86 to 6.84 for exact history,
on the intermediate pair $h=0.05$ to $h=0.025$.  The table also shows both
errors at $h=0.0125$.  For PECEC635 these are $8.33\times10^{-13}$ with
numerical startup and $9.44\times10^{-16}$ with exact history.  Their ratio
is arithmetically about 882, but the denominator is close to machine precision;
it is not a stable quantitative factor of startup degradation.  Reporting the
errors separately avoids that interpretation.  The three displayed fourth-order
controls have very similar intermediate slopes, while the fifth-order cases
show method-dependent error constants.  These slopes are finite
observations consistent with the bound $\min(p,q+1)$ of
Section~\ref{sec:architecture}; they do not prove the asymptotic order, and
the bound itself allows a higher observed order when the leading startup term
vanishes for a particular problem.  They do show that the default startup
does not in general deliver the nominal orders six and seven, and that more
accurate starting values are needed for that.

\subsection{Reproducibility and limits of interpretation}\label{sec:reprolimits}

The supplied figure generator rebuilds the plotted numerical panels.
The table generator rebuilds four specified tables, and a separate parser
builds Supplementary Table~S6 from the cross-check summary.  Other tables
are supplied as LaTeX with their associated records where available.  These
postprocessing operations are distinct from software execution.  The
supplied private snapshot and validation logs provide the latter evidence
within the environments and configurations identified in Section~\ref{sec:repro}.  The evidence
supports a common validated execution framework, a measurable action-fusion
transformation and reproducible, environment-specific work and timing
diagnostics.  It does not support superiority over external packages,
distributed-memory scalability, certified action-error bounds for every
stopping criterion, globally optimal automatic selection or a universal ETD34
efficiency factor.
